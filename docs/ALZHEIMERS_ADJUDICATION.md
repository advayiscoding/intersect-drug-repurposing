# Alzheimer strict-label adjudication

Research hypothesis, not medical advice. This tool does not tell anyone what to take. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist about any medicine.

I reviewed every rank-free drug–NCT–intervention occurrence in `artifacts/label_audit/alzheimers_sources.json` in source order against the rule frozen in `docs/STRICT_LABEL_RULE.md`. The source bundle represents the existing raw gate; each decision then applies the affirmative Alzheimer-participant and focal-drug clinical-objective gates. Missing or ambiguous affirmative evidence fails closed. The decision file preserves verbatim field-level registry evidence for every occurrence. These are labels for *studied interventions*, including clinical symptom studies, not claims of efficacy, disease modification, or a recommendation to take a drug.

## Difficult decisions

- **Mixed MCI and AD cohorts.** The detemir studies (NCT01547169, NCT01595646), sleep/wake modafinil (NCT00626210), montelukast (NCT03991988), Rapamune (NCT04200911), insulin aspart (NCT02462161), and the mixed glulisine trial (NCT02503501) all administer the focal drug to MCI and AD participants, but do not specify an AD-only clinical result. They are uncertain under the rule's mixed-cohort clause. Montelukast defines an early AD dementia group in eligibility, yet its registered outcomes remain pooled. By contrast, methylene blue (NCT02380573) has separate AD active and placebo arms and explicitly predicts memory-performance effects for the MCI and mild-AD groups relative to healthy controls; the AD contrast is identifiable. The healthy controls in the levetiracetam study (NCT03875638) receive no intervention, while the diagnosed AD group has its own active/placebo arms and neuropsychological outcome.
- **Prodromal AD versus risk alone.** Semaglutide (NCT04777409) requires an Alzheimer-type MCI/dementia syndrome plus amyloid positivity. Metformin (NCT01965756) requires MCI due to AD or early AD dementia with a positive AD-consistent imaging or molecular biomarker. Those meet the biomarker-supported prodromal pathway. Pioglitazone prevention (NCT01931566) enrolls cognitively normal at-risk people and is excluded. Its extension (NCT02284906) requires adjudicated MCI due to AD and excludes AD dementia, but this source does not establish biomarker support; that occurrence is uncertain. APOE4 risk alone in the choline study (NCT05880849) likewise does not establish a diagnosed disease population.
- **Separating components from regimens.** The doxycycline/rifampicin study (NCT00439166) registers single-drug, combination and matched-placebo arms, with Clinical Dementia Rating and ADAS-cog primary outcomes. Each focal antibiotic has a separable clinical contrast. The estradiol/medroxyprogesterone study (NCT00066157) crosses active/placebo patches and pills; the medroxyprogesterone pill has a separable contrast for registered cognition and functioning outcomes. Cilostazol (NCT01409564) varies against placebo while donepezil is common background. The simvastatin/tetrahydrobiopterin records (NCT01439555) contain only a three-drug regimen and no component-specific clinical comparison, so both are excluded as co-administered components.
- **Clinical outcome amid mechanism or safety work.** The pioglitazone pilot (NCT00982202) explicitly asks how cognition, daily function and behavior respond, with those registered as secondary outcomes, despite a safety primary endpoint. Riluzole (NCT01703117) explicitly evaluates cognitive functional change alongside imaging. The 3TC pilot (NCT04552795) names disease-relevant outcomes and registers PACC-5 dementia severity. Dapagliflozin (NCT03801642) explicitly says it will explore cognitive performance and registers ADAS-Cog 14 among other outcomes; this is a close call for the independent reviewer because its primary endpoint is magnetic-resonance spectroscopy. Isotretinoin (NCT01560585) emphasizes initial safety in its summary, yet its registered *primary* measure is change on ADAS-cog; that primary clinical test supports inclusion. In contrast, the semaglutide mechanistic study (NCT05891496), nicotinamide tau study (NCT03061474), sirolimus PET study (NCT06022068), telmisartan atrophy study (NCT02085265), baricitinib basket trial (NCT05189106), salsalate safety/PK study (NCT03277573), TW001 study (NCT05323812), atrophy-focused escitalopram trial (NCT00702780), and pimavanserin safety extension (NCT03118947) do not establish an explicit disease-relevant clinical-effect objective under the frozen rule. An incidental or exploratory clinical measure alone was insufficient.
- **Symptom studies.** Prazosin and citalopram for agitation, methylphenidate and modafinil for apathy, escitalopram for depression, and trazodone, zolpidem, and suvorexant for sleep qualify when their participants have the required AD diagnosis. A sleep or behavioral outcome need not claim disease modification. The other modafinil sleep study (NCT00626210) remains uncertain because its AD and MCI participants share the outcome.
- **Disease tags without disease recipients.** Olanzapine, risperidone and aripiprazole in NCT00245206 are studied for antipsychotic metabolic adverse effects in a broad DSM-IV disorder population; no AD clinical cohort is required or separately evaluated. Itraconazole (NCT06586606) and digoxin (NCT00831506) are healthy-volunteer interaction/PK agents. Scopolamine (NCT02051335) is a cognitive challenge agent in healthy adults. The escitalopram/memantine study (NCT01876823) expressly excludes dementia or probable AD despite discussing AD in its summary.

The record-level JSON is the operative audit. A separate rank-blind reviewer should check the above close calls and every source span before any evaluation. No ranking positions, aggregate performance measures, or outside sources were used in this adjudication.
